# ITIL Foundation Exam

The ITIL Foundation exam is the entry-level certification covering the ITIL 5 framework, designed for anyone who needs a shared language for IT service management (ITSM). Passing it shows you understand the vocabulary, concepts, and structure of ITIL — useful in interviews and day-to-day work with service teams. Exam details, prices, and certification paths change over time, so verify current information on peoplecert.org before you book.

## Who it's for

| Audience | Why they take it |
| --- | --- |
| Service desk / support analysts | Need a common language with the rest of the IT organization |
| Administrators & engineers | Understand how their work fits into service delivery |
| Project managers | Align project work with service value streams |
| Students | Enter the job market with a recognized ITSM baseline |
| Consultants | Credibility when advising clients on service management |
| Anyone touching IT services | Foundation assumes no prior ITIL certification |

## What's tested

Exact syllabus weightings change between versions and revisions — **verify the current syllabus on peoplecert.org**. Broadly, the Foundation exam covers:

| Topic | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| ITIL 5 overview & key concepts | What ITIL is, purpose, key terminology |
| Guiding principles | The 7 principles and how they apply |
| Service Value System & value chain | How value is created; the value chain activities |
| Four dimensions | Organizations & people, information & technology, partners & suppliers, value streams & processes |
| Key practices & their purpose | What each included practice is for (not deep mechanics) |
| Key terms & definitions | Precise meanings you must recognize on the exam |

## Prerequisites

None. Foundation is the entry point — no prior certification or work experience is required. If you already hold ITIL 4, a "Foundation Bridge (Version 5)" module exists to move you to ITIL 5 — verify current availability and requirements on peoplecert.org.

## Study plan (4 weeks, adjust to your pace)

```text
Week 1: Read the official book cover to cover — no notes, just structure.
Week 2: Re-read with notes on every term and definition; build a one-page term sheet.
Week 3: Practice questions daily; review every wrong answer and record why it was wrong.
Week 4: Full mock exams under timed conditions; sweep your weak areas until they are stable.
```

## Study tips

| Tip | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Make your own one-page term sheet | Writing definitions yourself beats re-reading |
| Understand the purpose of each practice, not just names | Exam questions describe scenarios, not labels |
| Practice questions under time pressure | 40 questions in 60 minutes needs pacing |
| Read every answer option | Partial-knowledge traps reward the first plausible option |

## What the certification does and doesn't prove

- **Does:** a shared vocabulary, awareness of the framework, a signal to employers of baseline knowledge.
- **Doesn't:** prove you can run an incident bridge, tune an SLA, or improve a broken process. Experience and concrete examples do that.
- Treat Foundation as the starting line, then combine it with hands-on practice — see [ITSM Roles & Career](itsm-roles.md).
